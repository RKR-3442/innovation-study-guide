CHAPTER 12

🌐 Innovation Offshoring & Global Networks

⭐ HIGH VALUE 🧠 UNDERSTAND GINs 📌 APPLE EXAMPLE
Offshoring = CAST benefits + GINs + Modularization enables it + Apple iPod = perfect example

MEMORIZE: Why Offshore R&D? — CAST Benefits

Mnemonic: "CAST"

C-A-S-T
  • Cost savings (lower wages, cheaper facilities in developing countries)
  • Access to global talent (specialized skills not available locally)
  • Speed — follow-the-sun R&D: 24/7 development across time zones
  • Tap into local knowledge (proximity to emerging markets, local R&D ecosystems)
Global Innovation Networks (GINs)
  • Definition: International networks of firms, universities, and research institutions collaborating on innovation
  • Key feature: Knowledge flows across borders — not just production, but R&D
  • Example: Silicon Valley → Bangalore → Shanghai knowledge networks
Modularization — The Key Enabler
  • Definition: Breaking complex products/processes into independent modules with defined interfaces
  • Why it enables offshoring: Each module can be developed independently by different teams globally
  • Interface specification: Teams only need to agree on interfaces — internal module design is independent

UNDERSTAND: The Apple iPod Case

Why Apple iPod = Perfect offshoring example:

Apple designed iPod architecture in California. Components sourced from: Japan (hard drive — Toshiba), South Korea (memory chips), Taiwan (LCD), USA (battery). Final assembly in China (Foxconn). Total value: $299. Apple's share: ~$80 profit. Manufacturing countries: fraction. Shows how modularized global production works.

Key insight: The highest-value activities (design, software, marketing) stayed in USA. Low-value manufacturing was offshored. This is "smile curve" — value at both ends (design + brand), not in manufacturing middle.

ActivityLocationValue
Design & ArchitectureApple, CaliforniaHigh
Hard DriveToshiba, JapanMedium

If asked about offshoring: use CAST for benefits, explain GINs as the mechanism, use Apple iPod as the example. Distinguish offshoring (same company, different country) from outsourcing (different company).

Most Likely Exam Questions

Explain how modularization enables innovation offshoring — use a real-world example▼
Full Question: "Modularization is the key enabler of innovation offshoring." Discuss this statement. Explain the concept of Global Innovation Networks (GINs) and analyze the benefits and risks of offshoring R&D activities. Illustrate with a real-world example.

Model Answer (10 marks)

[1. Modularization Concept - 2 marks]

Modularization = breaking complex system into independent modules with standardized interfaces. Each module can be developed independently as long as interfaces are respected. This is what allows different teams in different countries to work simultaneously without constant coordination.

[2. How Modularization Enables Offshoring - 2 marks]

  • Without modularization: complex interdependencies require co-location
  • With modularization: teams in different countries work on their modules independently
  • Interface specs enable coordination without co-location
  • Enables "parallel development" across time zones — 24/7 innovation

[3. Global Innovation Networks (GINs) - 2 marks]

GINs are international collaborative networks for innovation combining firms, universities, and research institutions across multiple countries. Unlike supply chains (physical goods), GINs transfer knowledge, expertise, and innovation capabilities. Key characteristic: knowledge flows in both directions (not just from HQ to subsidiary).

[4. CAST Benefits - 2 marks]

  • Cost: Lower R&D wages in India (~1/4 of US rates)
  • Access: 5M+ software engineers in India unavailable in USA
  • Speed: 24/7 development — US designs by day, India codes by night
  • Tap: Local market knowledge for products targeting emerging markets

[5. Apple iPod Example - 1 mark]

Apple iPod: Architecture in California, components from Japan/Korea/Taiwan, assembly in China. Modularized design allowed each component to be sourced from the world's best/cheapest supplier. Apple retained high-value design and marketing, offshored manufacturing and component production.

[6. Risks - 1 mark]

Knowledge leakage, IP theft, coordination costs, quality control, dependency on foreign suppliers, geopolitical risks (supply chain disruption).

Common Confusion: Offshoring vs Outsourcing

Offshoring

Same company, different country. Apple's own team in China. Control maintained. IP stays within firm.

Outsourcing

Different company (external supplier), can be same or different country. Less control. IP risk higher.

Can combine: "Offshore outsourcing" = external supplier in different country (e.g., Foxconn for Apple)
